# Wildloom tree art direction

**Capture the essence of a species first, then support it with botanical representation.**

The target is expressive, stylized-naturalistic vegetation for an illustrated game. A tree should feel designed as a whole before its leaves are inspected. Botanical detail supports that design; it cannot rescue an anonymous crown.

## The standard

1. **Silhouette.** Give each species a distinctive, appealing outline at normal forest distance. Judge it without labels, textures or lighting. A new leaf atlas alone does not establish a new species identity.
2. **Gesture.** Choose the dominant movement: spreading, rising, cascading, twisting, drooping, arching or reaching. Carry that movement through trunk, scaffold and crown. Variation should bend a coherent idea, not scatter unrelated parts.
3. **Mass hierarchy.** Compose the large crown first, its secondary groupings next, and leaf detail last. Use connected, overlapping masses with different importance. Avoid both isolated blobs and a featureless solid shell.
4. **Rhythm and negative space.** Alternate broad and narrow intervals, overlap and openings, dominant and supporting limbs. Use asymmetry deliberately. Repeated Y forks, radial symmetry, shelves and uniform foliage distributions are warning signs.
5. **Expressive exaggeration.** Strengthen the feature that makes the species readable: a lateral oak limb, a hanging willow curtain, a palm feather, a living root arch. Exaggeration must improve recognition, not create fantasy anatomy or overwhelm the whole tree.
6. **Cohesive color.** Keep species-specific color families within one environmental light treatment. Preserve rich interior shadows, restrained upper highlights and subtle variation between crown masses. Do not assign random bright colors or paint every surface gold.
7. **Botanical credibility.** Treat real growth habits, attachment patterns and leaf arrangements as constraints. Roots meet substrate; foliage belongs to supporting growth; cacao scaffolds transition from a trunk; palms retain palm architecture. Art-directed dimensions and gestures are not measured traits, abundance or ecological suitability.

## A small review loop

Write the species essence in one sentence using [the profiles](tree-species-essence.md). Block gesture and primary masses with the existing generator. Check the unlit silhouette and bare skeleton, then add the existing foliage detail. Review front and three-quarter views before committing to micro-detail.

Use a fixed structural seed and separate appearance seed. Compare variants under identical light and cameras; label individually normalized views. Also show a shared-world-scale view, so small trees do not secretly become giant canopies. Keep approved models as references. A higher leaf count is not an art-direction argument.

Score each review in plain language on recognizability, silhouette appeal, gesture/rhythm, mass hierarchy and botanical credibility. Record weak points honestly. Make one bounded experiment, capture the comparison, and stop for visual approval. Do not automatically polish away the evidence.

## Boundaries

These are artistic goals, not a new runtime system or universal anatomical rules. Age, exposure and growing conditions can change a species' form substantially. Profiles do not add native ranges, habitat suitability or abundance. Approved oak and Douglas-fir work remains protected. Root/branch intersections and atlas surfaces may still require later polish; hide neither limitation with camera changes nor extra foliage.
