# Reference-to-Recipe: observations before implementation

Actual image interpretation is available. Inspected: previous checkpoint and inspection sheet, supplied wide savanna and flowing-grass concepts, prior diagnosis and revised critiques. Capturing images and computing pixel metrics are separate capabilities; this analysis uses visible content.

## Complete-composition observations (direct image evidence)

1. **Luminance/color masses:** a connected warm field occupies most of the lower image; broad cooler olive/sage interruptions frame and turn it. Bright straw strokes sit inside these regions. This is not a brown plane with gold objects on it.
2. **Light/shadow relationship:** dark foreground interiors support illuminated grass tops and the bright middle; dark shapes are clustered, not attached as equally strong black outlines to every blade. Rock faces and nearby vegetation participate in the same light relationships.
3. **Depth:** foreground blades/rocks are large and overlap; midground vegetation is organized as sweeps/bands around a clear opening; distant trees and geological layers compress detail, contrast and saturation. Expansive sky is balanced by weight near the lower edges.
4. **Terrain and direction:** visual rolling comes from both long curved vegetation/value sweeps and gradual land silhouettes. Not every visible roll implies a terrain bump. Flows vary slowly enough to unify groups, but do not become parallel equal stripes.
5. **Massing/silhouettes:** broad acacia crowns and low grass swathes contrast in scale. Fine blades decorate crest boundaries; repeated radial fans, little opaque pillows and isolated chips are absent.
6. **Transitions:** plant bases merge into cool ground values; exposed soil is bordered by progressively shorter/sparser growth and overlapping blades. The opening reads as part of the same grassland.
7. **Anchors:** a few weathered rocks and overlapping grass establish asymmetric foreground weight and physical scale. Their effect depends on overlap, burial and adjacent shade, not just the presence of stones.
8. **Atmosphere:** warm near surfaces contrast with cooler, quieter distance; foreground clarity is maintained. Reference geology is layered, irregular and subordinate, not a blank beige wall.
9. **Frequency:** connected broad values survive thumbnail reduction; intermediate brush/grass sweeps carry most character; sharp small marks are localized. Lower detail everywhere would erase grass identity.
10. **Painterly language:** sky and land use related selective edges and long-form decisions. Materials differ while their visual rhythm agrees.

## Inferred techniques (hypotheses, not visible facts)

Shared world-space pigment/growth/lighting fields may coordinate these relationships. Diffuse painted surface detail can establish grass identity before geometry. Smooth connected grass fibres may supply silhouette/parallax without opaque mound facets or individual card shadows. Clustered, partially buried rocks can act as parents for local soil and growth changes. Existing distant geometry needs a material that connects to atmospheric land values. None of these techniques is proven by a reference image; staged renders must test them.
